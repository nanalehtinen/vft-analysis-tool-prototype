Prototype of a Verbal Fluency Task Scoring Tool

Based on the validated administration and scoring protocol of Lehtinen et al. (2023).

This is a prototype of a research tool for a partially automated scoring workflow for verbal fluency task (VFT) performance, based on the Instruction Manual for Administration and Scoring Verbal Fluency Tasks published in:

Lehtinen, N., Luotonen, I., & Kautto, A. (2023). Systematic administration and analysis of verbal fluency tasks: Preliminary evidence for reliable exploration of processes underlying task performance. Applied Neuropsychology: Adult, 30(6), 727–739.

The scoring protocol includes the total score, errors, temporal parameters, and clustering and switching measures for naturally occurring clusters.

The tool uses automated scoring for components that are fully specified by deterministic rules. Decisions requiring semantic or linguistic interpretation are presented to the researcher for manual scoring through a structured interface.

Data are processed locally and offline (on-device), supporting GDPR-compliant data processing.

Please see the Guidance tab for a walkthrough of the tool and the Scoring Rules tab for scoring guidance and the full scoring manual.

        Guidance

        How the workflow runs and how to read the results.

        Workflow

        The workflow has seven setup steps, followed by scoring for each trial. Results are presented grouped together with single trials visible.

        1. 1
          Data languageDetermines the scoring rules used for clustering.
        2. 2
          Task typeDetermines the clustering and switching rules applied.
        3. 3
          Task promptThe instruction given to the participant, stored with the results.
        4. 4
          Data formatTranscripts or voice recordings. Voice recordings are excluded from this prototype.
        5. 5
          Task-discrepant clusteringInclude or leave out clusters in the other domain. Scored automatically in semantic trials and manually in phonemic trials.
        6. 6
          Number of trialsA single trial, or a batch of trials from one participant group answering the same prompt.
        7. 7
          Import dataImport transcripts or voice files. Demonstration data can be loaded for easy access.
        8. 8
          Automated analysisAutomated results for all trials, and the measures that still need manual scoring.
        9. 9
          ClusteringPhonemic clustering is automatically scored by rule. The researcher marks semantic clusters by dragging across adjacent words for each trial.
        10. 10
          Group resultsMean, SD, range and n for each measure, with cluster tables for each trial. Results can be downloaded as CSV or JSON for analysis in R, SPSS or Python. Download is disabled for this prototype.

        Reading your results

        • Total score. Number of words produced, excluding errors.
        • Errors. Repetitions, categorical errors, paraphasias and nonwords, and language intrusions for multilingual populations, each with its error type.
        • Temporal parameters. Words produced per 15-second segment. Requires audio. Excluded from this prototype.

        Clustering is scored in two independent readings:

        • Task-congruent clustering. Clusters in the same domain as the task. Mean cluster size and number of switches are based on this reading.
        • Task-discrepant clustering. Optional. Clusters in the other domain, e.g. semantic clusters in a phonemic trial. Reported as a separate count.
        • Cluster tables. Every word in order of production, including non-clustering single words.

        Measures are summarized across trials in the Results tab. Cluster tables are shown for each trial.

        How scoring works

        Scoring combines automated rules with manual decisions by the researcher.

        1. Automated scoring. Total score, errors, temporal parameters and phonemic clustering for Finnish follow explicit rules in the manual. In validation on Finnish data, inter-rater agreement was excellent (ICC > 0.90) for phonemic cluster size and switches. Phonemic scoring rules for English are excluded from this prototype and will be added after their validation. In the final tool, the researcher can override automated results.
        2. Manual scoring. Semantic clustering is scored by the researcher. It requires judging whether words share a naturally occurring category, and showed lower inter-rater agreement in validation (ICC 0.75–0.90 for semantic cluster size).

        AI-assisted decision support (under evaluation). A feasibility study will test whether locally run multilingual models can support selected rule-application edge cases. Future studies may examine whether such models can also support semantic clustering. These features will be added only if the studies support them.
